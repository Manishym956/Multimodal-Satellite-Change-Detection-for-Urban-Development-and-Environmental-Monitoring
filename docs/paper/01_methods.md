# Section 3: Study Design and Methodology

## 3.1 Research Questions and Problem Formulation

Urban change detection from satellite Earth observation is critical for municipal tracking, infrastructure monitoring, and land-use assessment. Bitemporal optical satellite imagery (e.g., Sentinel-2) provides high spatial and spectral resolution, but operational monitoring is frequently disrupted by persistent cloud cover, haze, and adverse illumination. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR, e.g., Sentinel-1) provides all-weather, day-and-night active microwave imaging, but suffers from speckle noise, geometric distortions (layover, foreshortening, and shadow), and sensitivity to surface roughness and soil moisture fluctuations that do not represent structural change.

This study investigates **asynchronous multimodal satellite change detection**, wherein bitemporal SAR observations ($T_1$ and $T_2$) are combined with historical, pre-change optical imagery ($T_1$) to detect urban surface changes under the realistic operational constraint that contemporaneous post-change optical imagery ($T_2$) is unavailable.

We investigate five research questions (RQs):
- **RQ1 (Historical Guidance Utility):** Can historical Sentinel-2 optical imagery improve Sentinel-1 SAR-based change detection when contemporaneous post-change optical imagery is unavailable?
- **RQ2 (Temporal Gap Sensitivity):** How does the temporal separation between historical optical imagery and the target SAR observation affect change detection performance?
- **RQ3 (SAR Polarization Contribution):** Can additional SAR polarization information (VV vs. VH) improve discrimination between structural changes and environmental pseudo-changes?
- **RQ4 (Comparative Architecture Hierarchy):** How does the asynchronous multimodal architecture compare against unimodal (SAR-only, optical-only), early fusion, and synchronous dual-stream baselines?
- **RQ5 (Operating Conditions and Error Modes):** Under what spatial and observational conditions does historical optical guidance provide performance benefits, and where does it fail?

## 3.2 Dataset Descriptions and Label Construction

Experiments are conducted on two distinct benchmark scenarios. Cross-dataset performance figures are not directly comparable due to differences in spatial resolution, sensor preprocessing, geographic coverage, and label definitions.

### 3.2.1 Scenario 1: Onera Satellite Change Detection (OSCD)
The OSCD benchmark comprises Sentinel-2 optical imagery and coregistered Sentinel-1 SAR composites across 24 global cities. 

- **Optical Imagery:** Level-1C top-of-atmosphere reflectance Sentinel-2 imagery with 13 spectral bands ($B_1$ to $B_{12}$, including $B_{8A}$) resampled to 10 m spatial resolution.
- **SAR Imagery:** Sentinel-1 C-band SAR Level-1 Ground Range Detected (GRD) intensity in Interferometric Wide (IW) swath mode, single polarization (VV). The SAR data are multi-temporal composites generated across orbital cycles.
- **Labels:** Binary ground-truth change masks ($y \in \{0, 1\}$) annotated manually by remote sensing experts, indicating true urban changes (e.g., new building construction, earthworks, and road expansion). Natural seasonal variations in vegetation and water bodies are annotated as unchanged.

### 3.2.2 Scenario 2: SEN12-MT Urban Mapping (SpaceNet 7 Sites)
Scenario 2 evaluates a multimodal extension of the SEN12-MT Urban Mapping dataset, covering 28 geographically distinct SpaceNet 7 regions of interest.

- **Temporal Pair:** A fixed bitemporal pair spanning **March 2018 ($T_1$)** to **December 2019 ($T_2$)**, representing a uniform **21-month temporal separation** across all evaluated sites.
- **Optical Imagery:** Sentinel-2 Level-2A bottom-of-atmosphere surface reflectance, containing 10 spectral bands ($B_2, B_3, B_4, B_5, B_6, B_7, B_8, B_{8A}, B_{11}, B_{12}$) resampled to 10 m spatial resolution. In asynchronous configurations, $T_2$ optical data are strictly withheld.
- **SAR Imagery:** Sentinel-1 IW GRD bitemporal observations in dual polarization: Vertical transmit / Vertical receive (VV) and Vertical transmit / Horizontal receive (VH) for both dates.
- **Binary Change Proxy Derivation:** Ground-truth change annotations are not directly surveyed in this dataset. Instead, binary change proxy labels are derived by applying an XOR operation between bitemporal building-probability maps generated by deep building footprint segmentation models:
  $$\hat{y}_{i,j} = \left(\text{Building}_{T2}(i,j) \ge 0.50\right) \oplus \left(\text{Building}_{T1}(i,j) \ge 0.50\right)$$
  where $\hat{y}_{i,j} = 1$ indicates a transition between non-building and building status (or vice versa). These labels serve as proxy indicators of structural building development, not general land-cover or environmental transitions.

## 3.4 Preprocessing, NaN Handling, and Input Channels

### 3.4.1 Preprocessing and Normalization
- Optical reflectances are normalized into $[0.0, 1.0]$.
- SAR backscatter intensities are normalized into $[0.0, 1.0]$. In Scenario 2, raw SAR tiles contain invalid nodata pixels represented as NaNs resulting from sensor orbit boundaries and swath edges. During preprocessing, NaNs in SAR arrays are mapped to zero (`nan_to_num(x, nan=0.0)`).
- Concurrently, an exact binary pixel validity mask $M(i,j)$ is constructed:
  $$M(i,j) = V_{T1}(i,j) \land V_{T2}(i,j)$$
  where $V_t(i,j) = 1$ if and only if neither the VV nor the VH channel contains NaN at observation time $t$.

## 3.5 Model Architectures and Parameter Counts

All deep neural architectures were verified against saved model definitions and checkpoint state dictionaries:

1. **Dual-Stream U-Net (CNN):**
   - Comprises two parallel encoder streams: a SAR encoder and an Optical encoder. Each encoder employs five double-convolution stages with batch normalization, ReLU activations, and max-pooling ($64, 128, 256, 512, 512$ feature channels).
   - In the bottleneck, deep spatial representations from both modalities are concatenated.
   - A single shared decoder reconstructs the spatial resolution using bilinear upsampling, skip connections from both modality encoders, and final $1 \times 1$ convolution generating logit predictions.
   - **Clean Asynchronous CNN:** The optical encoder branch receives exclusively the historical $T_1$ bands, bypassing the requirement for contemporaneous $T_2$ optical observations.
2. **Transformer Cross-Attention:**
   - Designed for tokenized feature interaction with a compact parameter footprint.
   - Patch embedding layer with patch stride 4 maps SAR and optical inputs to an embedding dimension $D=128$.
   - Two self-attention blocks process SAR and optical tokens independently.
   - A cross-attention module queries the historical optical tokens: SAR patch tokens act as **Query ($Q$)**, while historical optical tokens act as **Key ($K$)** and **Value ($V$)**:
     $$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$
   - A lightweight convolutional decoder upsamples token representations to full spatial resolution.

## 3.6 Loss Functions and Class Imbalance Handling

Change detection in satellite imagery suffers from extreme class imbalance, where unchanged background pixels vastly outnumber change pixels.

### 3.6.1 Standard Binary Cross-Entropy with Positive-Class Weighting
For pixel logit $z_{i,j}$ and binary target $y_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\}$, the unreduced weighted BCE loss is:
$$\ell(z_{i,j}, y_{i,j}) = - \left[ w \cdot y_{i,j} \cdot \log \sigma(z_{i,j}) + (1 - y_{i,j}) \cdot \log (1 - \sigma(z_{i,j})) \right]$$
where $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$ and $w$ is the positive-class weighting factor (`pos_weight`).

- **OSCD Loss Configurations:**
  - All 6 CNN baselines (SAR-only, Optical-only, Sync Fusion, Sync Dual-Stream, Naive Async, Clean Async) were trained using **standard unweighted BCE** ($w = 1.0$).
  - Both Transformer baselines (Self-Attention, Cross-Attention) were trained using weighted BCE with **$w = 41.0$** (derived from the training set pixel ratio: 126,312 change / 5,146,327 no-change $\approx 1:40.7$).
- **Scenario 2 Loss Configuration:**
  - All baseline runs used weighted BCE with **$w = 94.0$** (derived from training pixel ratio: 50,387 change / 4,718,348 no-change $= 93.64 \to 94.0$).

### 3.6.2 Masked BCE Formulation (`MaskedBCEWithLogitsLoss`)
In Scenario 2, invalid nodata pixels in SAR rasters introduce zero-filled artifacts. Under standard BCE, these zero-filled pixels are treated as valid unobserved terrain and contribute to the gradient. Under Masked BCE, loss is evaluated strictly over valid pixels:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{masked}} = \frac{\sum_{i,j} M(i,j) \cdot \ell(z_{i,j}, y_{i,j})}{\sum_{i,j} M(i,j)}$$
where $M(i,j) \in \{0, 1\}$ is the validity mask. If a batch contains no valid pixels ($\sum M = 0$), the loss safely reduces to $0 \cdot \sum z$ to preserve gradient connectivity without computing NaN.

## 3.7 Training Pipeline and Deterministic Sampling Controls

- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $0$), initial learning rate $\alpha = 10^{-4}$.
- **Patch Sampling:** 64 crops per site per epoch, crop size $32 \times 32$ pixels. Batch size 64. Total budget: 50 epochs.
- **Scenario 2 Matched Multi-Seed Protocol:**
  To eliminate spatial sampling and initialization confounds across seeds 43 and 44, a deterministic crop coordinate schedule was pre-generated based on the verified spatial dimensions of all 20 training sites:
  $$\text{top}_{i,k} \sim \mathcal{U}(0, H_i - 32), \quad \text{left}_{i,k} \sim \mathcal{U}(0, W_i - 32)$$
  For each seed, both the standard-BCE and masked-BCE models were initialized with identical parameter weights ($\Delta \theta = 0.0$) and trained on identical sequences of spatial crops in identical order.
- **Checkpoint Selection Criteria:**
  - **OSCD:** Highest validation F1 score evaluated on full scenes of the 4 validation cities.
  - **Scenario 2 Baselines (Seed 42):** Highest validation F1 on full scenes of the 4 validation sites under the All-Pixel Protocol (`val_f1_all`).
  - **Scenario 2 S2-1 & Multi-Seed (Seeds 43 & 44):** Predeclared as highest validation F1 on the 4 validation sites under the **Valid-Pixel Protocol** (`val_f1_valid`). Both `val_f1_valid` and `val_f1_all` were recorded at every epoch.

## 3.8 Evaluation Metrics and Protocol Definitions

Predictions are generated by applying a threshold $\tau$ to predicted probabilities $p_{i,j} = \sigma(z_{i,j})$:
$$\hat{Y}_{i,j} = \mathbb{I}(p_{i,j} \ge \tau)$$
Evaluations are computed from pooled confusion counts across all test sites: True Positives ($TP$), False Positives ($FP$), False Negatives ($FN$), and True Negatives ($TN$).

The performance metrics are defined as:
$$\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}, \quad \text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$
$$\text{F1-score} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} = \frac{2 \cdot TP}{2 \cdot TP + FP + FN}$$
$$\text{Intersection over Union (IoU)} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP + FN}$$
$$\text{False Alarm Rate (FAR)} = \frac{FP}{FP + TN}$$

### Dual-Protocol Evaluation in Scenario 2
1. **All-Pixel Protocol:** Evaluates all pixels across full scene rasters, including nodata boundary pixels.
2. **Valid-Pixel Protocol:** Evaluates strictly pixels where $M(i,j) = 1$.

### Thresholding Protocols
- **Primary Threshold:** Fixed default threshold **$\tau = 0.50$**.
- **Secondary Validation-Calibrated Operating Point ($\tau^*$):** A sweep over $\tau \in [0.01, 0.99]$ in increments of $0.01$ is performed exclusively on the pooled validation scenes to identify $\tau^*$ maximizing validation F1. The resulting $\tau^*$ is then applied to the test scenes as an empirical operating-point diagnostic. Test labels are never accessed during calibration.
