Genuine strengths, briefly: the paper's process is unusually transparent. It uses pre-registered decision rules, 10 paired seeds, validation-only selection, stated deviations, and repeated statements that nulls are not equivalence. It reports results that go against its own framing (Wilcoxon p 0.049, async 1-month deficit). Using OSCD's own date-1 image as the "historical" image in RQ1 is a best case for the hypothesis, which makes the RQ1 null more informative, not less.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's nulls come from how the study was built, not from what historical optical imagery can or cannot do. DS-UNet fuses its streams with one linear 1×1 convolution over final features. The optical stream receives [pre-change image, zeros] and never sees SAR. So the historical image can add at most a SAR-independent, per-pixel logit offset. It cannot act as semantic context that changes how SAR evidence is read, which is the mechanism Song et al. propose. A null on RQ1 is therefore close to built in. The paper itself shows that the async model scores better when the image is removed.

RQ2 is worse. It is not a null, and the stated conclusion contradicts the paper's own Step 8 table. The registered test is a straight-line slope. The data are non-monotone: there is a 1-month dip, and the 6- and 18-month images are lower than the 3- and 12-month ones. A repeated-measures test over the five timings on the paper's own per-seed values gives p ≈ 0.001 for B and p < 10⁻⁶ for async. Timing has a detectable effect. It is just not linear. Every 1–12-month image also falls inside the labelled change interval. The "historical image" therefore mostly does not precede the change, so "Song's decline is not reproduced" has no evidential weight.

The RQ1 primary contrast is also fragile. Under the frozen clean threshold the paper uses in §5, B without the post-change image beats the SAR-only U-Net by +0.060 F1 (paired p 0.003, 8/10 seeds). The opposite verdict sits in the paper's own numbers.

What remains is a confirmation of Hafner et al. (RQ4), and a cloud study whose practical lesson is "mask clouds and treat them as missing", which is already standard practice.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Late-fusion bottleneck (RQ1, RQ2, RQ5).** An optical stream that never sees SAR, joined by a linear 1×1 convolution, can only act as an additive prior. Early or feature-level fusion of the historical image is the obvious control and is already in the codebase as input fusion. This is more parsimonious than "OSCD changes are visible in SAR".
2. **Too little training data for optical context.** With 10 training cities, a stream fed only [o1, 0] can memorise city appearance. The async model improving when its only optical input is removed (both thresholds) fits overfitting better than "no information".
3. **Threshold noise from 4 validation cities.** Two legitimate validation-only threshold rules differ by about 0.05 F1 on B@no_o2, more than every RQ1–RQ3 effect. Threshold-free metrics would separate signal from operating point.
4. **Seasonal or phenological mismatch (RQ2).** The ordering 3 / 12 months above 6 / 18 months fits a season-match effect that the linear slope cannot detect.
5. **Model switching instead of one dropout model.** A + SAR fallback, selected by known image availability, is never compared with the recommended B.
6. **Image-level discard instead of pixel masking (clouds).** rb99 ≈ "drop the image" matches or beats the pixel-masking operating points at ≥ 30% cover.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Operational users who pay per false alarm (municipal permitting, enforcement): precision and FAR, not F1.
- Users in persistently cloudy and tropical regions, where real cloud, haze and shadow dominate (only Rio and Chongqing among the test cities, and Chongqing is excluded from Step 8).
- Practitioners who need calibrated probabilities rather than a threshold tuned to a region.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes OSCD can host an "asynchronous / historical optical" question. Under OSCD's two-date labels, the only image that is truly pre-change is the date-1 image of the pair. So RQ1 reduces to "the standard bitemporal pre-image with the post-image missing", and RQ2 to "mid-interval images", as shown above. The asynchronous premise of the RRD (an older optical image as context for a new SAR observation) is never instantiated. That is why most headline answers are nulls or confounded.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The RQ2 slope CI (−0.0021 to +0.0187 F1 per year) does exclude a Song-sized *linear decline* (about −0.06 per year) for this setup. That is a real, narrow result the authors could state positively, alongside C1's correction.
- The use of OSCD's own date-1 image is a best case for RQ1. A null under the best case is informative within the architecture limit of M2.
- The within-B present-minus-absent contrast (−0.010 / −0.003 at two thresholds) is the paper's strongest RQ1 evidence and should be promoted.
- The paper consistently refuses to read nulls as equivalence in its prose. M7 is the one place where the practical conclusion slips into equivalence.
