## 5. Interpretation (H)

- **Dilation fixes edge errors.** When the detector finds every cloud but misses its edges, a recall-biased mask recovers
  most of the oracle performance for the dropout-trained model: retained gain rises from {RG0_erode4} to {RGS_erode4} for
  erode4 at 30% cloud, and the remaining cost relative to the oracle is under 0.05 F1.
- **Dilation cannot fix missed clouds.** Dilation only reaches a missed cloud that lies within r* of a detected one,
  and most missed clouds evidently do not. Retained gain is unchanged, and the cost relative to the oracle stays at {O_miss10} to {O_miss50} F1 at c30. This is what
  the pre-registration expected (§1).
- **Over-masking is only cheap for a dropout-trained model.** For A, which never saw zero-filled optical input in training,
  dilating a mask that already covers most clouds (the missed-cloud masks) zero-fills more clean image and lowers F1, and no
  eroded-mask gain survives correction. In these results, recall-biased masking helps only together with modality-dropout
  training.
- **Consequence for Step 5 (real detector).** The value of recall bias for a real detector depends on its error type: errors
  at cloud edges and thin margins should be largely recovered; whole missed clouds will not be. Step 5 measures which
  dominates for s2cloudless on real cloud pixels.

## 6. Limitations

- **Synthetic on both sides.** The clouds are real catalogue shapes filled with the flat Phase 3 spectrum, and the detector
  errors are morphological (erosion) or whole-cloud removal. Neither is a real cloudy acquisition or a real detector.
- **Shared radius at the grid limit.** One r* for all variants, levels and arms, chosen on 4 validation cities whose masks
  were built the same way as the test masks, and at the edge of the grid.
- **Seeds are not geography.** The 10 seeds are training replicates evaluated on the same 10 official OSCD test cities.
- **Zero is the fill value used here,** applied to standardised inputs; it is not intrinsically a missing-data token.
- **Power.** A null is not equivalence (section 2).
