# Phase 3: post-change optical cloud corruption (simulated reflectance, real cloud shapes)

Protocol: `PHASE3_PREREGISTRATION.md`, written before evaluation. The tables at the end are generated by
`analyze_phase3.py` (`phase3/tables.md`) and pasted here unchanged. **V** marks a finding supported under the
pre-registered rule; **H** marks an interpretation or hypothesis.

![curves](phase3/curves.png)

## 1. Integrity checks

- All 20 Phase 2 checkpoints were evaluated once per condition, unchanged.
- Anchors passed for every checkpoint:
  - `clean` test counts equal the Phase 2 `full` counts, at the stored threshold and at 0.5;
  - `missing` test counts equal the Phase 2 `no_o2` counts;
  - the clean validation argmax equals the stored threshold.
- All runs share one set of source hashes (`phase3/runs/*.json`), and these match the pre-registered `eval_phase3.py` (after the documented pre-test fix) and `corrupt_post` code.
- Deviations, both before any test evaluation, are documented in the pre-registration:
  - the mask build was redone with a relative tolerance;
  - a missing `model.eval()` was fixed after a validation-only smoke test.
- Test evaluations added by this phase: one pass per checkpoint over 7 conditions. No threshold, mask or parameter was chosen from test data.

- **V (rule met):** B > A at every partial cloud level, in 10/10 seeds, Holm p < 0.05. The `missing` row replicates Phase 2; it is not new evidence.
- **But the effect is small next to the damage.** Both arms lose most of their F1 under cloud:
  - Even **10% cloud cover costs A about 0.27 F1 and B about 0.22 F1**.
  - At 30–60%, both arms are close to an "everything is change" detector, whose F1 is ≈ 0.098 (the change prevalence).
  - The dropout advantage at the partial levels is +0.044, +0.026 and +0.011 F1.
  - The honest summary: **modality dropout does not make the model robust to clouds.** It only degrades slightly less.
- **For B, a partly clouded post image is far worse than no post image.** At the clean threshold, B's F1 on `missing` (0.478) is far above its F1 at 10% clouds (0.329).
  Dropout taught B to ignore *zero-filled* optical. It did not teach B to ignore *cloud-valued* optical.

## 3. Failure mode (V, descriptive)

- **Clouds are read as change.** At the clean threshold, almost every pixel under a cloud is predicted as change:
  - false-alarm rate inside the mask rises from about 0.03–0.05 to **0.96–1.00** for both arms;
  - recall inside the mask goes to ≈ 1 only because everything there is flagged.
- **This is not lost evidence.** Recall outside the mask stays at or above its clean value.
- **False alarms also spill outside the mask.** Outside-mask false-alarm rate rises:
  - A: 0.025 → 0.058 / 0.134 / 0.199 at c10 / c30 / c60;
  - B: 0.042 → 0.052 / 0.075 / 0.095.

  This is consistent with the U-Net's receptive field carrying the cloud signal into nearby clear pixels. B spills less (**H**).
- **Haze and full cloud (secondary).**
  - **Haze** (α = 0.3 over the whole post image): A collapses to all-change at the clean threshold. B degrades less (Δ +0.067, Holm p 0.016 in its own secondary family).
  - **c100** (every post pixel set to the cloud spectrum): both arms flag everything as change.

## 4. Threshold adaptation (secondary)

Re-selecting the threshold per condition on cloud-corrupted validation cities recovers part of the loss. The B − A
differences at the partial levels are then no longer Holm-significant: c10 Holm p 0.11, c30 0.27, c60 0.27.
The validation thresholds come from only 4 cities with their own cloud masks, so they are noisy. In any case a deployed
model would not know the cloud level, which is why the frozen clean threshold is primary.

## 5. What this means for the next step (H, not tested here)

- **Partial cloud is the specific failure.** The model must not treat cloud-valued pixels as evidence of change. B already
  handles zero-filled post optical well, so the simplest remedy needs no new architecture:
  1. detect clouds (a cloud mask, e.g. s2cloudless or the scene's own QA);
  2. zero-fill the post optical **only under the mask**;
  3. train dropout with **spatial, partial** zero-fill masks, not whole-image dropout only.
- Two variants of that remedy are cheap tests and should be pre-registered before they run. Both can reuse these masks and checkpoints:
  - **(a) evaluation-only:** zero-fill inside the cloud mask with the existing checkpoints, assuming an oracle mask;
  - **(b) retraining:** use spatial dropout masks during training.
- A reliability-gated architecture is justified only if (a) and (b) still leave a specific failure, for example spill-over
  around cloud edges, or dependence on a perfect cloud mask.

## 6. Limitations

- **The corruption is simulated.**
  - Clouds are opaque and uniform, with hard edges and no shadows, built from one fixed spectrum.
  - Real clouds have texture, thin edges and shadows, and they change the atmosphere around them.
  - The masks are real cloud shapes, but they come from other scenes.
- **Opaque clouds are an extreme case.** Thin cirrus, or a residual mask from an atmospheric correction, could behave differently. The haze condition is the only partial-transparency test.
- **Only the post-change date is corrupted** (decision 1).
- **The 10 seeds are not independent test sets.** They measure training variability on one set of 10 test cities. The p-values do not support claims about other regions or sensors.
- **B was not trained on clouds.** The comparison asks whether whole-image dropout transfers; it does not test a cloud-aware method.
