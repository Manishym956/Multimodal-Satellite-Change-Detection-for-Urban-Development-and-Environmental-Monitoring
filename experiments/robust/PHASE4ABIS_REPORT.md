## 3. Secondary results (each its own Holm family, descriptive)

- **B at 10% and 60%:** the same pattern. Dilation costs ≤ 0.007 F1. Erosion and missed clouds cost up to -0.315 (miss50 at 60%).
- **A full-only:** A's oracle gain is small to begin with (F1 0.261 oracle vs 0.165 cloud at 30%), and **dilation hurts A
  more than B** (-0.011/-0.022/-0.038 at 30% for radii 2/4/8). On over-masked clear pixels, A's false-alarm rate rises from
  ≈ 0.024 to 0.385–0.497: A reads zero-filled clear pixels as change. B's rises only from ≈ 0.040 to
  0.047–0.065, but B's recall on those pixels falls (0.716 → 0.615, dilate4 at 30%).
- **B − A at 30%:** B > A under every imperfect mask, by +0.074 to +0.283 F1, in 10/10 seeds.
- **Error regions:** on missed-cloud pixels, B's false-alarm rate is 0.909–0.962 for every miss and erode4/8 variant
  (≈ 0.04 on the same pixels with clean input). Unmasked cloud pixels are almost all predicted as change. erode2 is lower
  (0.603–0.633), because its missed pixels are thin cloud edges next to masked pixels.
- **Other thresholds:** at 0.5 and at the per-condition validation threshold, B's pattern at 30% holds. Every dilation costs
  < 0.02 F1 relative to the oracle under the same threshold rule, and every erosion and missed-cloud variant costs at least
  0.045 F1 (the smallest is miss10 at threshold 0.5).
  The full per-threshold values are in `phase4abis/summary.json` (`arms.<arm>.0.5`, `arms.<arm>.level`).

## 4. Interpretation (H)

- **The masked-zero-fill strategy degrades gracefully in one direction only.** For B, a conservative detector that over-masks
  costs almost nothing. A detector that leaves cloud pixels unmasked loses F1 as the missed fraction grows, more slowly at large
  fractions (erode8 misses 0.431 of the cloud pixels and costs 0.243). Per missed pixel, edges and whole clouds cost similar amounts,
  with edges slightly more (erode2 misses 0.116 → −0.100 and miss10 misses 0.100 → −0.090; erode4 0.232 → −0.187 and miss25 0.250 → −0.168).
  This is consistent with Phase 3: any cloud-valued pixel that reaches B is read as change.
- **Practical implication, under this simulation only:** a cloud detector used with B should favour recall over precision
  (e.g. a dilated mask). This is an inference from synthetic errors, not a tested detector.
- **For Step 4B:** the remaining failure is in the unmasked cloud pixels, not in the zero-filled ones. Spatial
  partial-dropout training (zero-fill inside cloud shapes) teaches the model to handle zeros. It does not directly teach it to
  ignore cloud-valued pixels, so 4B should not be expected to fix the erosion/miss cost. Whether it does is an open empirical question.

## 5. Limitations

- **The mask errors are synthetic** (morphological erosion/dilation and whole-cloud removal). They do not model a specific
  cloud detector, and real detector errors also involve thin cirrus, cloud shadows and bright surfaces mistaken for cloud.
- **The corruption is the simulated Phase 3 corruption:** opaque clouds, one fixed spectrum, no shadows, post-change date only.
  It is not equivalent to real cloudy acquisitions.
- **Zero is the fill value used here, not an intrinsic missing-data token.** B treats it as missing because Phase 2 training
  showed it zero-filled images.
- **The 10 seeds are training replicates on the same 10 test cities,** not independent geographic samples.
